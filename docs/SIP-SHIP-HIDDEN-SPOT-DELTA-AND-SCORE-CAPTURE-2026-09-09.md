## Transcript-only hidden spots beyond the earlier pass

### 1. Problem-first storytelling behaves like an implicit scoring multiplier

Across the calls, the strongest spontaneous reactions tend to arrive when the builder starts with a real human problem, ideally personal, then shows the product. Verilox and Other Me are especially clear examples.

NimCarry action: open with the human failure of warm introductions, not blockchain architecture.

### 2. Live proof consistently beats technical explanation

NimJump's technical explanation was cut short in favor of seeing the game and its replay proof. Concept-only or blocked demos generated materially less excitement than visible working behavior.

NimCarry action: the judge should see `Create -> Invite -> Accept -> Pass 1 NIM -> FINAL -> ARRIVED` before hearing about Postgres/HMAC/RPC internals.

### 3. "Simple / intuitive / crystal clear" functions like an implicit meta-rubric

The host repeatedly uses these terms when an app lands well, while added complexity is explicitly described as making evaluation harder.

NimCarry action: five-verb judge flow, <10 second comprehension target, <60 second core point.

### 4. Emergent real-user behavior is treated as PMF evidence

When Nimiq Space showed users creating things the builder did not expect, Martin explicitly called it a small sign of product-market fit.

NimCarry action: a few genuine completed routes with consenting bridges are more valuable than another speculative feature.

### 5. The competition itself learned that ecosystem utility was underweighted

Cycle I included a live debate in which Chuck argued that protocol-utility tools were disadvantaged by the original marketing-heavy scorecard. Richie agreed that protocol utility deserved high valuation. Cycle II then materially shifted to 45 functionality/reliability/usefulness + 25 Nimiq integration.

NimCarry action: do not chase "hype app" mechanics. Deep native Nimiq utility is now much more directly rewarded if it is visible and understandable.

### 6. Do not lead with the word "crypto"

The Stakes builder explicitly said the blockchain should sit underneath the experience; Martin strongly agreed that crypto carries baggage and should not be the front-door message.

NimCarry action: human routing / bridge / destination on the front door; Nimiq becomes explicit exactly at authorization, FINAL and receipt proof moments.

### 7. Judges can only reward what they can see and understand

Cycle I builders were warned that invisible submission/marketing evidence cannot be scored. In Cycle II Call #3, Martin explicitly told Nimword that an educational/ESL angle could improve perceived value if communicated clearly.

NimCarry action: never assume the judge will infer target user, differentiation, why 1 NIM matters, or why FINAL changes custody. Put those claims in the product/demo/submission.

### 8. Financial-looking mechanics are scrutinized immediately

Ajo triggered a quick rules check because the contribution-circle mechanic could sound like chance/gambling. Across the corpus, "stake," rewards, pools and prizes are highly salient language.

NimCarry action: proactively say `1 NIM is the baton, not a reward, stake, wager, prize or pooled fund.`

### 9. Never require the judge or host to cooperate for core proof

Nimico tried to make the host participate as the second player; setup consumed the demo window and the core interaction never landed cleanly.

NimCarry action: use controlled demo accounts/state. The real product remains multi-human, but the core judge proof must be deterministic.

### 10. Lifecycle/re-engagement gaps are noticed quickly

Memory Vault immediately received a question about how recipients know when something becomes claimable.

NimCarry action: after a handoff, users need a simple answer to `what is waiting for me?`, `what happens if I decline?`, and `did the mission arrive?`. `My Routes` is justified after E2E if it does not destabilize the core.

### 11. Once the core is understood, distribution and abuse become the next questions

NimHunt quickly moved from concept understanding to partnership/distribution and GPS-spoofing concerns.

NimCarry action: make the natural bridge-invite loop and consent/sender/finality/loop protections easy to explain without turning the demo into a security lecture.

### 12. The best demos let the observer map the app to their own life

Rally and Blacktop generated strong reactions when Martin immediately imagined personal use cases.

NimCarry action: demo one concrete, credible warm-introduction mission that a judge can instantly imagine needing.

### 13. Ecosystem-reusable primitives attract unusually deep technical interest

NimConnect/on-chain handles generated a much deeper technical discussion around SDK reuse and cross-app integration than most isolated apps.

NimCarry action: preserve a future seam for reusable route/receipt verification primitives, but do not expand scope before Cycle II proof.

## Cycle II easy-point capture — explicit Sep 9 guidance

Martin describes the Builder Promotion Checklist as essentially free/easy points and says everyone should get them:

- **2 points** — one post in the Mini Apps Competition Skool community.
- **3 points** — one real public social-media post about the Mini App.
- **Total: 5/5 builder-promotion points.**

These should be treated as a must-capture score floor, not as optional marketing work.

### Real-usage buckets

The Sep 9 scoring explanation also makes usage highly discrete:

- `0-3` unique users -> **0/15**
- `4-10` -> **6/15**
- `11-24` -> **10/15**
- `25+` -> **15/15**

No bots, artificial wallets or gaming. Suspected manipulation can produce a zero/disqualification.

### Practical score-floor engineering for NimCarry

After secure E2E, the low-cost path is:

1. Skool post -> +2.
2. Public social post -> +3.
3. Get at least 4 genuine unique wallet opens -> +6 usage.

That produces **11 relatively low-friction points outside the 80 points of core product/integration/UX**.

Then:

- reaching 11 genuine users raises that outside-core total to **15 points** (`5 promotion + 10 usage`);
- reaching 25+ genuine users raises it to the full **20 points** (`5 promotion + 15 usage`).

The strategic hidden spot is not merely "market more." It is **score-floor engineering**: secure the deterministic low-friction points immediately once the app is safe enough for real users, then let every testing action also improve reliability, UX and usage at the same time.

## NimCarry execution consequence

Do not spend time on promotion before the secure vertical flow is real. Once E2E is green:

`Skool post + public post + first 5 observed testers -> 4+ unique opens -> 11-point floor -> 11+ users -> 15-point floor -> 25+ users -> full 20/20 promotion+usage.`
